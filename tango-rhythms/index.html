Section 02 · Milonga patterns

Five ways to place the steps

From Thanh Dang's Tango Theory — The Milonga Rhythms. Same four-beat pulse every time; what changes is where the steps land. He draws two timelines for each pattern: symmetric (steps on the beat) and a-symmetric (one of the counts is a sincopa, an off-beat step). His a-symmetric timelines draw the ticks unevenly, which reads as if the pulse itself moved — it doesn't. The grid stays even; only the step is displaced. Then he says "Test Yourself" and stops talking, so you can hear it without him.

Section 02

Tempo is the speed. Rhythm is the shape.

Two songs can share a tempo and feel nothing alike, because rhythm is the pattern of accents laid over that pulse. Turn the tempo slider on any card — the shape stays, only the urgency changes.

Tempo

The ticking pulse

Measured in beats per minute. It sets how much space you have per step: slow tempo, long strides and time to decorate; fast tempo, compact steps and quick reactions.

Typical: tango ≈ 60–70 BPM on the walking beat · vals ≈ 160–190 counting every beat (or 55–65 felt as one pulse per bar) · milonga ≈ 90–120 per main beat.

Rhythm

The shape on top

The grouping of notes, the accents, the gaps. It decides where you could step, where you should step, and where you deliberately don't.

Elasticity: tango high (rubato, silences, frozen steps) · vals moderate (steady enough to keep rotation flowing) · milonga low (locked like a clock, that's the bounce).

Section 05

Orchestras as rhythm teachers

Once you hear the three grooves, you start hearing how each orchestra handles tempo — who pushes, who stretches, who never lets go.

D'Arienzo

Staccato drive

The rhythm section is the melody. Sharp marcato, clean accents, almost milonga-like energy — the classic “rhythm is everything” school.

Di Sarli

Elegant and even

Steady, unhurried beat with a lush melodic line above it. Great for learning to walk on the pulse instead of chasing the notes.

Pugliese

Rubato and drama

Slow, dense, enormous stretches and silences. The lesson in elasticity: the tempo is a suggestion, the tension is the point.

Biagi · Troilo · Canaro

Play and colour

Biagi bounces, Troilo turns on a dime, Canaro pours the vals. Different accents over the same three skeletons.
